Recycling electronic devices remains a partial and fragile solution, undermined by design choices that favour replacement over care, maintenance and repair. As a result, In France alone, between 54 and 110 million still functional smartphones lie forgotten in people’s drawers. But these undead objects still hold potential for invention. TechnoZombies aims to bring back into circulation outdated intergenerational devices, and repurpose them as instruments of resistance and imagination. It develops a post-obsolescence toolkit: borrowing from practices like lightweight coding, circuit bending, hardware modification, and alternative operating systems, opens a way for technological disobedience. It starts with six smartphones - officially declared as socially obsolete by software  some of their components that got brocken (battery, camera, sound output, screen  -, some intercoms and some toys and appliances parts, treasure hunted in second-hand shops in their well-known electronic sections.Countering obsolescence involves a phase of experimentation: opening the sealed shell of a smartphone, reorganizing the pins of a mini-USB cable,  retroengineering to bypass proprietary communication protocols, connecting old devices through local communication protocols and opensource tools to create a 4-part music synthesizer (the ZombieChoir) as a first zombie. It also requires sharing of practices: It includes an online lightweight blog and repository presenting tests and techniques, with the goal of becoming a platform for sharing practices, middle tech solutions and hacks to build your own zombie. Obsolescence is not a natural outcome of progress. It is a designed condition. Bringing old media back to life (or alternate forms of life) is about reclaiming the right to maintain, reconfigure, repurpose, care and imagine new uses, even (more) when systems are engineered to exclude these.
